Ember · Loom · the model, v2

Not a layout. A grammar.

The first round asked "which layout?". That's the wrong question, because whichever layout we pick will be wrong half the time. Loom has no fixed layout. It has a small set of rules for how the agent's work becomes things you can see, point at, question and steer. The screen then becomes whatever this moment needs, and nothing jumps when it changes.

01

Everything is an object

Every finding, file, number, diff, decision or mock the agent produces is a live thing. You can focus it, open it, ask about it, act on it, pin it and trace where it came from. Text is just the cheapest view of an object.

02

The screen is composed, not chosen

The agent says what it wants you to see ("compare these", "you need to decide this"). Loom composes it under fixed perceptual rules, and you can override it at any time. Objects morph between arrangements, so you never lose track of them.

03

The keyboard is the spine

Every object is two keystrokes away. Every verb is shown for whatever is focused, and the prompt is always at the bottom. The mouse speeds you up but is never required, so it still feels like a terminal.

02What the research says about people

Each rule in Loom comes from a finding about how people perceive, remember, attend and feel in control. None of this is taste. It's the floor that makes "fluid" possible without becoming chaos.

Working memory holds ~4 things

People hold about four chunks at once, not seven.

Now shows at most 4 focal objects. Everything else is a one-line chip, one key away.
Cowan 2001, BBS

Overview, zoom, details on demand

The visual information-seeking mantra: see the whole, narrow down, then open one thing.

Atlas and Story are the overview. ⏎ opens details. Lenses give the depth.
Shneiderman 1996

Information scent

People forage: they follow cues that predict what's behind a link, and leave when the scent fades.

Every chip carries its scent (4.8s, +9 −23, 42/42), so you know before you open it.
Pirolli & Card 1999

Object constancy

Animated transitions between views measurably improve tracking what changed.

Objects never cut between spaces, they morph. The same card goes from chip to river node to map node.
Heer & Robertson 2007

Spatial memory is fast

Stable spatial layouts beat menus once learned. People remember where things are.

Regions are stable, and pinned objects stay exactly where you put them. Loom never reflows behind your back.
Scarr et al. 2012, CommandMaps

Recognition, with a path to recall

Novices need to see options. Experts need to skip them. Marking menus showed one design can train the first into the second.

The bar shows the verbs for what's focused. You read it at first, and later your fingers just know.
Kurtenbach & Buxton 1993

"Put that there"

People naturally point and say "this". Deixis is much faster than describing.

Whatever you select becomes "this" in your next sentence, attached as an exact object, not a guess.
Bolt 1980, MIT

0.1 s · 1 s · 10 s

Under 0.1 s feels instant, 1 s keeps flow, and past 10 s attention leaves.

Every local action answers in <100 ms. The agent's slowness is shown in the periphery, and text never waits for a visual.
Card, Robertson & Mackinlay 1991

Mixed initiative

Good assistants weigh the cost of acting against asking, and let the user take over gracefully.

The agent proposes the composition. You override. Loom remembers your overrides per object type.
Horvitz 1999, CHI

Interruptions cost most mid-task

Interrupting at task breakpoints is far cheaper than in the middle of one.

In Free mode the agent never moves your view. Decisions queue as a badge until you reach a natural pause.
Iqbal & Bailey 2008 · Mark et al. 2008

Agency comes from initiating

User-initiated interactions strengthen the sense of agency, and agency loss is mostly a design problem, not a user one.

You start, the agent responds. Everything can be undone or forked, and nothing irreversible happens without a visible decision.
AMCIS 2026

Calibrated trust

People over- or under-trust automation unless they can see how it reached its result.

Every object has a provenance lens showing which tool calls, files and turns produced it.
Lee & See 2004

Generative UI wins, if not slow

Human raters strongly prefer interfaces generated for the question over plain text, when generation time is ignored.

Generated lenses only when the built-in ones don't fit. They bloom in after the text, and never block it.
Google Research 2025 · Leviathan et al. 2026

Many views per object

Moldable development: every object should show itself in several views fitted to context. One study had 131 custom views averaging about 9 lines each.

Lenses: view, raw, provenance, related, plus type-specific ones. New lenses are cheap and can be saved.
Chiș et al. 2015 · Glamorous Toolkit

Calm technology

The best tech moves between the periphery and the center of attention, and stays out of the center until it's needed.

The agent's progress lives at the edges (the pulse, the river head). The center is for meaning.
Weiser & Brown 1995

Ladder of abstraction

Understanding comes from moving fluidly between the concrete and the abstract.

Semantic zoom, from chapters to turns to every tool call, without losing your place.
Bret Victor 2011

This is the one primitive everything else is built from. The interpreter's job, described in round 1 (declared → structural → inferred), is to turn the CLI's output into these.

Addressable

It has a stable id and a hint letter. It's two keystrokes from anywhere, and it's the same object in every space.

Inspectable

It has several lenses. The default view is chosen by type, and you can cycle with l. Raw is always there, so nothing is ever hidden.

Askable

Ask a scoped question in a popover. It runs in a forked side session with this object's provenance as context. Promote the answer, or drop it.

Actionable

Verbs depend on type: a diff can be applied, reverted or blamed; a metric re-run; a mock varied; a decision chosen. Each verb is a prompt or keys sent to the real CLI.

Pinnable

A pinned object stays where you put it while the composition around it changes. That's your spatial anchor.

Traceable

Every object knows its turn. Scrubbing time shows the world as it was, and forking from there is a real --fork-session.

04Three spaces, three kinds of memory

These aren't three apps. They're the same objects arranged three ways, matching how memory actually works. You switch with one key and the objects fly to their new positions.

SpaceMemoryQuestion it answersArrangement
1 NowworkingWhat matters right this second?≤4 focal objects composed from the agent's current intent. The rest are chips along the spine (the terminal-honest event list).
2 StoryepisodicWhat happened, and in what order? What if we'd gone the other way?A river along time. Forks are real parallel sessions. [ ] scrub, and rewind is Esc Esc at the playhead.
3 AtlassemanticWhat do we know? How do the pieces relate?A mind map built automatically. Entities (files, functions, routes, findings, decisions) come from tool calls, and relations from co-occurrence and provenance. It grows across sessions per project, like Ember's Map.

05How the agent directs the screen

The agent doesn't write layout. It states a presentation intent, either declared through the extended ember-panel tool or inferred by the interpreter. Loom maps that intent to a composition using fixed rules. That's what makes the screen fluid without being random: the same intent always looks the same.

IntentCompositionExample
focus(x)x large in center; its relations as chips beside"here's the file"
compare(a, b…)side by side, same scale, a diff highlight betweenbefore/after, two forks, 3 mock variants
sequence(steps)horizontal strip, current step lita migration plan, a request's journey
explain(flow)diagram with each node an object (askable)"how auth works here"
decide(q, options)decision card with keyed options and the evidence pinned beside it; badge if you're in Freepermission prompts, design choices
demo(url | video)live surface: the running app, a recording, a Playwright trace; scrubbable"it works, try it"
report(…)living document of goal, findings, decisions, resultend of a long task
generate(html)a bespoke generated lens, sandboxed, using Ember's tokens; can be saved as a reusable lensa calculator, a simulator, an interactive explainer

Overrides are learned without any model. If you keep opening diffs in split view, or keep moving metrics to the left, Loom counts that and makes it the default for that type. You end up shaping the interface just by using it.

06Control: you always have the wheel

Follow / Free

As with presenters in Figma: in Follow the agent moves the view to what it's showing. In Free you drive, and the agent's news waits at the edges. ⇧F flips it. Loom drops to Free by itself the moment you touch anything.

Feedforward, not just feedback

The agent's next steps (from its todo list) show as ghost nodes ahead on the river. You can see where it's going and veto a step before it happens.

Steer mid-flight

Type while it works and your message queues as a steering note it picks up at the next breakpoint. Esc interrupts, as in the CLI. The autonomy dial is Claude Code's own modes (⇧⇥), shown as a chip.

Everything reversible

Rewind to any node, or fork from it into a real parallel session. Exploration is only fun when it's free.

Nothing hidden

Raw is a lens on every object, and Ctrl+` is the real TUI of the same live session. If Loom misreads something, the truth is one key away.

Side questions stay on the side

Asking about an object forks a side branch, so your curiosity doesn't fill the main agent's context. Promote an answer when it matters.

09Things that would ruin it (and the guards)

The agent stealing your focus

Guard: Free is the default as soon as you interact. The agent may only move your view in Follow, and decisions queue.

Novelty fatigue from generated UI

Guard: built-in lenses first. Generated lenses use Ember's tokens and grammar, so they feel native, and they're never the only view: raw is always there.

Layout jitter

Guard: compositions come from intents with fixed rules. Pinned objects never move, and every move animates.

Lag

Guard: local actions under 100 ms. The model tier is async and upgrades in place. Side questions show streaming tokens at once.
